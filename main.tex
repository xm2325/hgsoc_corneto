\begin{abstract}
Patient-derived ovarian cancer models (OCMs) preserve clinically relevant
heterogeneity, but a network state inferred from a single cohort can reflect
patient repetition, tissue composition, prior-knowledge coverage, or a solver's
arbitrary optimum. We describe a reproducible, response-blind framework for
testing whether metabolic and regulatory states in high-grade serous ovarian
cancer (HGSOC) OCMs are cross-cohort reproducible and robust to these choices.
We harmonized 117 paired-end RNA-seq runs from four public studies and froze a
primary cohort of 60 tumour OCMs from 52 patients (9, 13, 11, and 27 models per
study). Signed transcriptional and signalling priors from CollecTRI and
OmniPath were contextualized with CORNETO/CARNIVALFlow, while the Meeson
constraint-integration algorithm was audited against global and joint metabolic
formulations using Human-GEM. As technical controls, NMF was run separately by
cohort and on a pooled 60-OCM matrix, with patient-balanced sensitivity
analysis.

The completed response-blind results show substantial but incomplete agreement
between pooled and cohort-stratified NMF states (rank-3 adjusted Rand index
0.377--0.723; mapped assignment agreement 0.727--0.889). A deterministic
patient-balanced analysis increased agreement to 0.942 (ARI 0.842). Regulatory
solution summaries were available for 45 pooled/cohort fits across nine
pre-specified regularization values; the patient-balanced pooled network had
union Jaccard 0.890 and mean per-sample Jaccard 0.899. A richer prior-knowledge
network reduced agreement (pooled union Jaccard 0.202; mean per-sample Jaccard
0.108), demonstrating that stable modules, rather than individual edges, are
the appropriate reporting unit. Meeson toy benchmarks independently showed
sequential-order sensitivity (retained-set Jaccard 0) and symmetric global
alternative optima. The four full-cohort metabolic jobs were still undergoing
solver/receipt validation at the manuscript snapshot and are not interpreted
as biological results. Exact OCM-linked paclitaxel AUC, GI50, and cumulative
exposure tables are unavailable; consequently this work does not claim drug
response prediction, intrinsic resistance, or acquired resistance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-grade serous ovarian cancer is molecularly heterogeneous and most patients
eventually relapse after platinum--taxane therapy. The Taylor-group living
biobank established patient-derived ex vivo ovarian cancer models (OCMs) with
extensive proliferative capacity and later expanded this platform to an
83-model drug screen\cite{nelson2020,tighe2025}. These models provide a controlled setting in
which molecular heterogeneity and drug response can be studied together, but
their usefulness depends on separating
tumour biology from cohort, passage, tissue-composition, and patient-repetition
effects. The public Taylor-group resources contain both tumour and non-tumour
material, as well as repeated models from some patients. Treating every RNA
library as an independent observation can therefore inflate apparent
replication.

Network contextualisation adds a second source of uncertainty. A signed prior
knowledge network (PKN) is incomplete and unevenly supported, transcription
factor (TF) activity is inferred rather than measured, and integer optimisation
can have multiple equivalent solutions. In metabolic modelling, the published
Meeson workflow proposes expression-derived reaction constraints sequentially;
the retained reaction set can depend on candidate order\cite{meesonrepo}. A global formulation
can remove this particular order dependence but can still have alternative
optima. In signalling, a multi-condition penalty can encourage a shared network,
yet the result depends on the PKN and the regularisation convention.

We therefore ask a deliberately narrower question than ``which edge causes
paclitaxel resistance?'':

\begin{quote}
Do HGSOC OCMs contain metabolic and regulatory states that reproduce across
cohorts, remain robust to repeated patients and modelling choices, are enriched
relative to stromal/reference material, and receive support from external
mechanistic evidence?
\end{quote}

The analysis contract separates four evidence layers. Primary tumour-only
models define the main estimand; pooled and patient-balanced fits test
robustness; stroma, non-HGSOC, and cell-line samples are reference contrasts;
and the Meeson/TPI1 analyses provide model-level mechanistic checks. Drug
response is intentionally downstream of inference. This separation prevents a
response value from influencing source selection, network construction,
regularisation, or stability tuning.

\section{Study design and data}

\subsection{Public RNA cohorts and the primary OCM universe}

We archived run-level metadata and paired-end FASTQ reports for four BioStudies
accessions: E-MTAB-7223, E-MTAB-10801, E-MTAB-11000, and E-MTAB-14568. These
studies support the living-biobank, subtype-classification, PARG-inhibitor, and
taxol/cabazitaxel--SUMOylation publications, respectively
\cite{nelson2020,barnes2021,coulsongilmer2021,tighe2025,littler2025}. The
committed manifest contains 117 RNA runs (234 FASTQ files; 444.951~GiB
compressed). Salmon quantification and one-reference aggregation were performed
on Roihu scratch storage, with run receipts and byte-level checks retained.
The primary analysis includes one representative tumour RNA library per OCM,
restricted to HGSOC and excluding stromal cultures, non-HGSOC or ambiguous
histotypes, cell-line controls, and duplicate later passages.

The 60 primary OCMs represent 52 patients: most patients contribute one model,
six contribute two, and one contributes three. All patient-grouped sensitivity
analyses use patient identity rather than OCM identity as the resampling unit.
The remaining runs are retained for quality control and explicitly labelled
reference categories; they are never silently pooled with the primary tumour
cohort.

\subsection{Phenotype boundary}

The prespecified phenotype outcomes are paclitaxel area under the curve (AUC)
as primary and raw GI50 in nM, analysed as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{GI50})$, as
secondary. The public supplementary tables available to this repository do not
contain machine-readable exact OCM-level AUC, GI50, or cumulative paclitaxel
exposure values. The frozen phenotype-intake gate therefore reports zero exact
rows and blocks all response association, intrinsic-exposure, and
acquired-resistance claims. Chemotherapy-naive-at-biopsy is retained as a
metadata field but is not treated as proof of zero cumulative exposure.

\section{Response-blind regulatory inference}

\subsection{Prior networks and TF activity}

CollecTRI signed TF--target interactions\cite{collectri} and OmniPath directed signed
interactions\cite{omnipath} were downloaded from public endpoints, normalised to remove
self-loops and ambiguous signs, and retained on scratch storage with URL,
timestamp, response metadata, and SHA256 receipts. For each sample, gene-level
expression was transformed as $\logtpm$. Signed regulon scores provide TF
activity evidence; they are not measurements of protein activity or causal
perturbations.

\subsection{CORNETO formulation and sensitivity grid}

The signalling model uses CORNETO's CARNIVALFlow formulation\cite{liu2019,corneto}: a signed directed
subgraph is selected to connect expression-supported TF outputs through an
OmniPath prior while trading fit against network size. We evaluated a
pre-specified nominal regularisation grid
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{reported}} \in
  \{0,0.001,0.01,0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2\}.
\]
For a multi-condition problem with $n$ included samples, the implementation
uses the equivalent solver penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{solver}} = n\lambda_{\mathrm{reported}}$
so that a reported value has the same meaning when the data-fit term is
expressed as a mean rather than a sum. Pooled, cohort-stratified, and
patient-balanced runs use the same source-policy contract and record the
condition set, network hashes, solver status, and blocked/error reasons.

The primary reporting objects are module recurrence, per-edge selection
frequency, solution ensembles, and patient/study-aware uncertainty. A single
edge selected by one optimum is not treated as a stable mechanistic discovery.

\section{Metabolic modelling and Meeson audit}

\subsection{Human-GEM fingerprint}

The metabolic track pins Human-GEM v1.4.1\cite{human1} (SHA256
\texttt{57d1b137f0c90d83a3e4f9a8225d74d37523594e6ee99f622b160a014d9f7050}),
with 13,096 reactions, 3,628 genes, a complete GPR partition, and the biomass
reaction SBML identifier \texttt{R\_biomass\_human}. The model-level audit maps
TPI1 to \texttt{ENSG00000111669} and reaction \texttt{HMR\_4391}; this is an
input/model check, not an OCM-specific knockout result.

\subsection{Sequential, global, and joint formulations}

For a sample $s$ and candidate reaction $r$, expression-derived bounds are
represented by retain variables $z_{sr}$. The Meeson-style sequential method
tests candidates one at a time and reopens a bound when the growth threshold
would be violated. The global formulation maximises simultaneous retention
under the same feasibility constraint. The proposed joint formulation adds a
shared indicator $u_r$ and a cross-sample penalty, so a reaction can be reused
across conditions:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{z,u}\; \sum_s E_s(z_s) + \lambda \sum_r u_r,
  \qquad z_{sr} \leq u_r,
\end{equation}
subject to stoichiometric steady state and sample-specific growth targets.
This is a new formulation built with CORNETO primitives; it is not described as
an exact reproduction of the published MitoCore sFBA implementation.

The four cohort full metabolic jobs use Human-GEM v1.4.1, primary tumour-only
samples, $\logtpm$, candidate budget 25, growth fraction 0.9, independent
$\lambda=0.1$, joint $\lambda=1.0$, and explicit Gurobi without fallback. At
the current manuscript snapshot their final receipts were still undergoing
content validation; consequently no cohort metabolic objective, flux, or
cross-cohort biological claim is included below.

\section{NMF and patient-balanced comparisons}

For technical state comparison, non-negative matrix factorisation was run on
the primary tumour expression matrices using the top 6,000 median-absolute-
deviation genes, ranks 2--10 where supported by cohort size, and 100 starts per
rank, following the consensus/stability logic introduced by Brunet et
al.\cite{brunet2004}. The pooled matrix contains all 60 primary OCMs; cohort matrices are
analysed independently. Rank-3 was retained as a pre-specified comparability
anchor because it was used by the existing 14568 integration, while rank-2
stability is reported as a sensitivity rather than silently replacing the
anchor. Cluster labels are aligned by maximum-overlap matching before ARI,
NMI, agreement, loading Spearman correlation, and study-association tests.

The patient-balanced analysis selects one deterministic representative per
patient (52 OCMs), preserving the study-aware input contract. This is a
sensitivity analysis, not a claim that one representative is biologically
privileged; a resampling distribution would be a stronger future analysis.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Cohort and reproducibility gates}

All four RNA studies passed strict quantification and aggregation gates. Matrix
columns, run receipts, sample QC, and manifest accession sets were aligned, and
the primary tumour counts were 9, 13, 11, and 27 (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}).
The primary cohort therefore supports a 60-OCM analysis while retaining 57
reference/excluded runs for explicitly labelled contrasts. No exact phenotype
intake table passed validation at the analysis snapshot.

\subsection{Pooled versus cohort-stratified NMF}

Pooled rank-3 solutions had cluster sizes 23, 20, and 17, with cophenetic
correlation 0.939 and silhouette 0.770. Rank 2 was technically more stable
(cophenetic 0.972; silhouette 0.899), so rank 3 should be interpreted as a
comparability anchor rather than a uniquely data-driven optimum.

The pooled state--study association was not statistically clear (chi-square
$p=0.207$, Cramer's $V=0.265$), although the non-zero effect size motivates
study-aware sensitivity. Patient balancing increased the rank-3 ARI to 0.842
and mapped agreement to 0.942, supporting technical robustness to the observed
repeated-patient structure while not eliminating cohort heterogeneity.

\subsection{Regulatory network robustness}

The completed response-blind regulatory summary contains 45 pooled/cohort
receipts across the nine nominal $\lambda$ values. The patient-balanced pooled
network retained high overlap with the corresponding 52-patient analysis
(union Jaccard 0.890; mean per-sample Jaccard 0.899). In contrast, replacing
the narrow prior graph with a richer PKN produced a pooled union Jaccard of
0.202 and mean per-sample Jaccard of 0.108; cohort union Jaccards were
approximately 0.108--0.143. Thus the evidence supports a stable core plus
uncertain alternatives, not an invariant list of individual signalling edges.

Regulatory--NMF integration was a descriptive same-input join. It is useful for
asking whether model-supported edges are distributed differently across
technical expression states, but it is not independent validation, subtype
discovery, or evidence of paclitaxel mechanism. The longitudinal summary covers
60 runs and eight within-family transitions; treatment-associated rewiring
remains blocked until paired exposure and phenotype data are available.

\subsection{Meeson toy benchmarks expose order and optimum ambiguity}

The public-code audit and toy tests provide a direct methodological result.
With two interchangeable routes and a shared uptake capacity, the two
sequential candidate orders produced opposite retained reactions and retained-
set Jaccard 0. The global MILP removed this iteration-order dependence but had
two symmetric optimal solutions (growth 6.000001; objective $-1$), one retaining
each route. In the two-condition joint toy, independent models activated a
four-reaction union (biomass, uptake, and both routes), whereas the joint model
used a three-reaction union by sharing one route across conditions. These tests
validate the intended computational mechanisms; they are not evidence from
human OCMs.

\section{Discussion}

This project separates three kinds of reproducibility that are often conflated:
agreement of expression-derived technical states, agreement of optimisation-
supported network hypotheses, and external biological validation. The NMF
results show that pooled and cohort-stratified structure is neither identical
nor completely cohort-specific. The patient-balanced increase in agreement
supports explicit control of repeated-patient weighting. The regulatory results
show that network conclusions are sensitive to PKN breadth, making module- and
ensemble-level recurrence more defensible than a single selected edge. The
Meeson toy demonstrates why sequential order and alternative optima must be
reported rather than hidden behind one output file.

The analysis does not yet justify a claim of a cross-cohort metabolic state.
The four full-cohort metabolic runs must first produce valid receipts, including
sample counts, solver status, growth targets, objectives, active-flux unions,
and provenance. Only then can cohort comparison, pooled-60 metabolic inference,
TPI1/FVA, and reference contrasts be interpreted. This fail-closed ordering is
important because a Slurm completion or a non-empty log is not a scientific
result.

The central translational question is also intentionally unresolved. Exact
paclitaxel AUC, GI50, and cumulative exposure are absent from the validated
input table, so response association, intrinsic resistance, and acquired
resistance prediction are not estimable from the current public snapshot. Once
these tables become available, the prespecified M0--M4 models (ABCB1/covariates,
expression, TF activity, CORNETO modules, and their integrated extension) must
use patient-grouped nested validation. Random OCM-level splits would leak
patient information.

\section{Limitations and planned analyses}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Expression is a proxy for activity; TF activity is inferred, and
  CORNETO paths are optimisation-supported hypotheses rather than causal
  posteriors.
  \item Public reference categories are unbalanced. Tumour--stroma contrasts
  should be run within accession where possible and reported with study-aware
  uncertainty; cell-line controls are descriptive QC only.
  \item The patient-balanced result is one deterministic selection of 52 OCMs.
  Patient-level bootstrap or repeated one-per-patient sampling is needed to
  quantify its uncertainty.
  \item PKN breadth changes regulatory overlap substantially. Stable cores,
  edge frequency, alternative-optima frequency, and multiplicity-controlled
  contrasts should replace simple set subtraction.
  \item Exact Meeson 49-OCM regeneration is blocked by the absent public
  expression-plus-growth input. The repository therefore distinguishes public
  algorithm auditing from exact numerical reproduction.
  \item No result in this manuscript establishes that a treatment caused a
  longitudinal network change; at most, future paired analyses can support a
  treatment-associated rewiring claim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Taylor-group HGSOC/OCM resource compendium}
\label{sec:compendium}

This section turns the manuscript into a citable working reference for the
Taylor-group ovarian-cancer programme and the public resources that enable the
present analysis. ``Taylor-group resource'' is used here in a project-specific
sense: the catalogue includes publications that generated, characterised, or
mechanistically interrogated the living OCM biobank, plus directly enabling
metabolic and network resources. It is not intended to reproduce all of
Professor Stephen Taylor's research output in unrelated systems. The
authoritative full career list (77 articles, two reviews, and one preprint at
the date accessed) and ORCID 0000-0003-4621-9326 are available through the
University of Manchester profile\cite{taylorprofile}.

\subsection{How the resource programme fits together}

The programme can be read as four connected layers. First, mechanistic studies
of mitotic fate, replication stress, TP53 loss, MYC, and drug efflux define
testable vulnerabilities. Second, the living biobank provides early-passage,
patient-derived tumour and matched stromal cultures with longitudinal and
inter-patient heterogeneity. Third, drug screens and transcriptomic studies
connect those models to PARG, ATR/CHK1, SUMOylation, taxol/cabazitaxel, subtype,
and HRD questions. Fourth, the Meeson studies add transcriptome-constrained
Human1 models and TPI1 perturbation evidence. The present CORNETO project joins
these layers computationally but keeps response-blind discovery, reference
contrasts, and phenotype validation separate.

\subsection{Question-to-resource reading guide}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{What is an OCM and how is the living biobank organised?}
  Start with Nelson 2020, then the Nelson 2023 review, and use Tighe 2025 Table
  S1 for the expanded model/patient index.
  \item \textbf{Which public RNA-seq files define the present 60-OCM cohort?}
  Use E-MTAB-7223, E-MTAB-10801, E-MTAB-11000, and E-MTAB-14568 together with
  the frozen master manifest; never infer primary eligibility from an accession
  alone.
  \item \textbf{How should tumour, stroma, cell-line, and non-HGSOC comparisons
  be interpreted?} Use 7223/10801 for within-study tumour--stroma contrasts,
  Kuramochi/FNE only as descriptive controls, and the Barnes histotype work for
  non-HGSOC context. Set overlap should be accompanied by effect sizes,
  uncertainty, and multiplicity control rather than ``present/absent'' claims.
  \item \textbf{What supports replication-stress and CIN mechanisms?} Read
  Pillay 2019, Bronder 2021, Coulson-Gilmer 2021, Golder 2022, and Nelson 2023;
  these provide mechanistic context but do not substitute for validation of a
  CORNETO-selected module.
  \item \textbf{What supports taxol/MDR1/cabazitaxel hypotheses?} Use Topham
  2015 for mitotic fate, Williams 2020 for ABCB1 structural activation, and
  Tighe 2025 for the 83-OCM screen. Exact model-level response association
  remains blocked until AUC/GI50/exposure values pass the intake gate.
  \item \textbf{What supports metabolic and TPI1 hypotheses?} Use Meeson 2024
  for the integration method, Meeson 2026 and E-MTAB-16770 for experimental
  TPI1 perturbation, the pinned PhD repository for code/output auditing, and
  Human-GEM for the model. The 49-OCM historical output is an external
  comparator, not an exactly regenerated result.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Known access and reproducibility boundaries}

The reference catalogue deliberately records negative availability statements.
The four OCM RNA studies are fully public and locally receipt-validated; exact
OCM-linked paclitaxel AUC, GI50, and cumulative exposure are not present in the
validated public tables. Tighe 2025 reports no original code. Exact regeneration
of the Meeson 49-OCM models is blocked by a missing expression-plus-growth input,
although the algorithm, model fingerprint, and final public flux table can be
audited. E-MTAB-16770 is public but answers a cell-line perturbation question,
not the observational 60-OCM question. Living cultures, some images, and
additional clinical annotations require controlled access or author contact.
These boundaries are part of the result: unavailable data are not reconstructed
from plots, inferred from labels, or replaced with proxies.

\section{Data and code availability}

The analysis contract, manifest, scripts, tests, source registry, status
register, and manuscript are maintained at
\url{https://github.com/xm2325/hgsoc_corneto}. Large FASTQ, regulatory-network,
Human-GEM, and solver outputs remain on project scratch storage when licensing,
size, or credentials prevent redistribution. Every derived receipt records
input identifiers and hashes without committing license files or secrets. The
English and Chinese pipeline status registers document which jobs are complete,
pending, blocked, or scheduler-only.
